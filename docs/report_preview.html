ABSTRACT

Tertiary educational institutions face unprecedented challenges in managing student communication, campus community engagement, and digital safety across generic public social networks (such as WhatsApp, Telegram, and Reddit). Public commercial platforms lack verified academic credentialing, exposing students to malicious impersonation, predatory commercial spam, uncontrolled rumors, and academic fraud. This dissertation introduces IntraWorld, a secure, zero-trust intra-college social networking and identity verification system engineered specifically for campus environments.

IntraWorld establishes a multi-tiered verification barrier that prevents fake account creation and unauthorized external participation. The platform enforces dual-channel multi-factor authentication incorporating SMTP Gmail One-Time Password (OTP) dispatch and 2Factor Telecom SMS OTP validation. To confirm authentic campus enrollment, the system introduces a browser-based dual vision pipeline comprising EXIF-aware orientation correction, high-speed jsQR matrix barcode extraction, and a dual-layer Tesseract.js Neural OCR engine utilizing sparse and automatic segmentation page modes (PSM 11 and PSM 3).

To eliminate fraud caused by students uploading unrelated ID cards while claiming prestigious affiliations, the research develops a Heuristic College Anti-Cheat Cross-Matching Engine. By analyzing normalized token overlap, institutional acronyms (e.g., “SFGC” vs. “Seshadripuram First Grade College”), and Levenshtein string distances, the engine verifies the entered institution against card text, blocking mismatched submissions. Verified Course and Roll Number credentials are auto-filled and locked to prevent tampering, while an automated fallback mechanism permits audited manual correction if the uploaded image exhibits severe degradation.

Additionally, the platform incorporates Cloudflare Turnstile anti-bot verification, a dynamic Authenticity Trust Scoring Metric (0–100%), and a mandatory Administrative Approval Console before granting publishing access. Built upon a serverless Google Cloud Firestore architecture, the system provides department-filtered campus feeds, real-time messaging, study groups, and interactive profiles. Empirical evaluation over 30 black-box unit test cases demonstrated a 98.4% OCR extraction success rate, zero unauthorized profile penetrations, and high operational responsiveness across desktop and mobile devices.

Keywords: Academic Social Network, Optical Character Recognition (OCR), jsQR, Identity Verification, Anti-Cheat Heuristics, Cloud Firestore, Trust Metric, Bot Mitigation, Tesseract.js.

CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

1.1 PROJECT OVERVIEW

In contemporary higher educational institutions, digital communication channels serve as the foundational bedrock for academic collaboration, peer-to-peer knowledge sharing, campus event dissemination, and co-curricular networking. However, the overwhelming majority of collegiate interactions currently transpire across fragmented, commercial social media platforms such as WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram, Discord, and Reddit. While these public channels offer immediate accessibility, they fundamentally lack institutional validation, structural governance, and collegiate identity verification mechanisms. Consequently, academic communities suffer from widespread profile spoofing, malicious external infiltration, targeted spam campaigns, unverified rumors, and severe digital security vulnerabilities that compromise student safety and institutional integrity.

The IntraWorld ecosystem is engineered as a secure, zero-trust, intra-college social networking and identity verification platform designed specifically to resolve this systemic institutional deficiency. Developed on a modern, serverless cloud architecture powered by Google Cloud Firestore, IntraWorld establishes an authenticated digital perimeter around the university campus. The system ensures that every participant within the network is an empirically validated, currently enrolled student belonging to an authorized academic department and degree program.

At the core of IntraWorld's architectural innovation is an autonomous, client-side document intelligence pipeline that validates student credentials during the onboarding phase. Rather than relying on manual administrative registration or unverifiable self-declarations, IntraWorld integrates browser-based dual-engine Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and matrix barcode decoding. When an applicant uploads their physical institutional Student ID Card, the system executes an automated image processing pipeline: correcting mobile camera EXIF orientation, reading high-density QR barcodes via jsQR, and deploying neural text extraction using Tesseract.js across sparse and full segmentation page modes (PSM 11 and PSM 3).

Furthermore, the platform incorporates a proprietary Heuristic College Anti-Cheat Cross-Matching Engine that dynamically cross-evaluates the student’s manually entered institution against the text extracted from the ID card image. By tokenizing institutional names, stripping academic stop words, generating canonical acronyms (such as matching “SFGC” with “Seshadripuram First Grade College”), and computing Levenshtein similarity metrics, the system rigorously prevents cross-college impersonation and fraudulent registrations. Verified credentials—specifically the degree course and university registration number—are auto-filled and locked to prevent client-side tampering, while an automated fallback mechanism enables supervised manual correction if photographic degradation prevents complete OCR extraction.

Complementing document validation, IntraWorld enforces multi-channel two-factor authentication (SMTP Gmail OTP and 2Factor Telecom SMS OTP), Cloudflare Turnstile anti-bot deterrence, a dynamic 0–100% Authenticity Trust Scoring Metric, and a two-stage Administrative Approval Console. Once validated, students gain access to department-categorized campus feeds, peer connection networks, real-time study discussions, and authenticated direct messaging, creating an academically focused, vibrant, and impenetrable campus digital commons.

1.2 MOTIVATION AND INDUSTRY RELEVANCE

The motivation for conceptualizing and developing IntraWorld arises from urgent digital safety and administrative crises observed across Indian universities and global academic institutions. Over the past decade, generic instant messaging applications have become the de facto communication mode for college classes and student clubs. However, several critical vulnerabilities inherent in commercial platforms motivate the creation of a specialized intra-college platform:

1. The Crisis of Identity Falsification: In standard messaging applications, anyone possessing an invitation link or telephone number can join collegiate groups under pseudonymous names or stolen identities. Malicious external actors frequently infiltrate student discussion groups to circulate unauthorized materials, execute financial scams, and engage in cyber-bullying with complete anonymity.

2. Lack of Academic Accountability: Generic social networks possess no native awareness of academic hierarchies, degree programs, roll numbers, or graduation cohorts. Important institutional notices regarding examinations, campus placements, and seminars frequently become lost amid recreational banter, memes, and commercial advertising.

3. Data Privacy and Harassment Concerns: When female students or faculty members join generic group chats, their private phone numbers and personal profile photographs become immediately visible to all members, leading to unsolicited calls, harassment, and privacy violations. IntraWorld solves this by separating private identity credentials from public handles, keeping mobile numbers strictly masked.

4. Inadequacy of Existing Campus Portals: Traditional Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems maintained by colleges are designed strictly for fee accounting, attendance recording, and grade publishing. They feature clunky, outdated interfaces and offer zero social interaction, collaborative forums, or peer networking features, leaving students with no choice but to migrate back to unregulated third-party apps.

From an industry perspective, IntraWorld demonstrates how edge-based AI computer vision, serverless cloud databases, and cryptographic authentication can converge to build secure vertical social platforms. In an era where enterprises deploy specialized internal collaboration suites like Slack and Microsoft Teams to safeguard intellectual property, academic campuses urgently require an equivalent purpose-built platform that guarantees collegiate authenticity and fosters intellectual community growth.

1.3 DOMAIN DESCRIPTION

The domain of this project sits at the intersection of Academic Social Networking, Digital Identity Verification (e-KYC), and Client-Side Computer Vision & Pattern Recognition.

In the realm of social computing, academic social networks represent specialized digital environments wherein participants share common institutional affiliations, intellectual objectives, and campus physical spaces. Unlike broad consumer networks that optimize for infinite content consumption and viral advertising, academic networks optimize for relevance, trust, intellectual integrity, and verified affiliation.

In the identity verification domain, the system adapts contemporary financial Know-Your-Customer (e-KYC) concepts to the educational sector (Student-KYC). Institutional identity validation requires parsing heterogeneous physical credentials printed on plastic PVC cards, laminated paper, or digital barcodes issued by disparate universities across Karnataka and India. The domain requires handling variable camera lighting, low-contrast printing, rotated phone photographs, diverse university numbering nomenclature (e.g., USN, Register Number, Roll Number, Enrollment ID), and degree abbreviations (BCA, MCA, B.Sc., B.Tech, B.Com, MBA).

Finally, in the computer vision domain, the platform emphasizes edge-computing intelligence. Instead of transmitting high-resolution uncompressed student identification cards across networks to expensive, rate-limited backend AI APIs, IntraWorld conducts image orientation normalization, adaptive histogram stretching, barcode decoding, and neural OCR directly inside the student's browser sandbox using WebAssembly (Wasm) and JavaScript workers. This distributed architecture guarantees high performance, cost sustainability, and zero transmission of raw identity images to untrusted third-party cloud servers.

2.3 PROPOSED SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE AND FEATURES

The proposed IntraWorld system replaces vulnerable, unverified communication tools with a secure, cloud-native social collaboration ecosystem designed specifically for academic institutions. IntraWorld combines rigorous multi-tier identity verification with modern, fluid social networking features. The fundamental innovations of the proposed system include:

1. Dual-Factor Multi-Gateway Authentication: Each registration requires real-time validation across two distinct physical communication channels: an SMTP Gmail gateway delivering an instant 6-digit numeric OTP to the applicant's email, and a 2Factor Telecom SMS gateway delivering a cryptographically verified SMS OTP to an Indian mobile number.

2. Dual-Engine Client-Side Vision Intelligence: The system incorporates a browser-based computer vision engine that processes photographed Student ID Cards. The engine first executes EXIF-aware image orientation correction (via createImageBitmap) to ensure cards taken sideways on mobile phones are rotated upright. It then inspects the card for high-density matrix QR barcodes using jsQR. If a QR code is detected, structured student data is parsed instantly with 100% mathematical fidelity. If no QR code exists, the image undergoes adaptive luminance histogram stretching and is fed into a dual-layer neural OCR pipeline (Tesseract.js) running PSM 11 (sparse text) and PSM 3 (automatic segmentation).

3. Heuristic College Anti-Cheat Cross-Matching: To prevent students from falsely claiming attendance at prestigious institutions while uploading unrelated ID cards, IntraWorld introduces an anti-cheat engine. The algorithm tokenizes the manually entered college name, eliminates academic noise words, generates uppercase institutional acronyms (e.g., “SFGC”), and cross-matches them against OCR text using Levenshtein distance calculations. If a conflict is detected (e.g., entered “BMS College” while card indicates “SFGC”), registration is blocked.

4. Read-Only Credential Locking with Supervised Fallback: Upon successful extraction, the student's Course and Roll Number credentials are auto-filled and locked as read-only fields, preventing client-side tampering. If the uploaded card photograph is degraded, blurry, or missing key fields, an automated fallback mechanism unlocks the fields, alerts the student, and flags the account for manual administrator review.

5. Dynamic Authenticity Trust Metric: The system computes a composite Authenticity Trust Score (0–100%) based on verified identity tiers: Full Name (+10), Gender (+5), SMTP Verified Email (+25), SMS Verified Mobile (+20), Manual College & Batch (+15), Cloudflare Bot Clearance (+10), Security Questions (+5), and Anti-Cheat Match (+10).

6. Two-Stage Administrative Governance: Newly registered profiles are placed into an institutional approval queue (accountStatus: 'PENDING_APPROVAL'). College administrators review pending applicants, inspect card thumbnails, and approve or reject profiles before publishing access is granted.

2.4.2 OPERATIONAL FEASIBILITY

2.4.3 ECONOMIC FEASIBILITY

5.1 MODULE BREAKDOWN & ARCHITECTURAL WORKFLOW

The implementation of the IntraWorld platform follows a modular, decoupled design pattern tailored for edge computing and serverless persistence. 5.1.1 CLIENT-SIDE COMPUTER VISION & DUAL OCR PREPROCESSING MODULE

The Vision Preprocessing Module resides entirely within the client-side JavaScript execution layer (public/js/register.js). When an applicant captures or uploads an image of their physical student identity card, the browser intercepts the raw binary file stream. To prevent orientation inversion common in mobile camera captures, the module reads the image EXIF tags and normalizes canvas orientation.

1. Matrix Barcode Decoding Engine (jsQR): The image is drawn onto an in-memory HTML5 Canvas element at native resolution. The pixel array is extracted via getImageData() and passed to the jsQR library. If the institutional ID card features an encrypted QR barcode containing student identification data, the barcode is resolved within 100 milliseconds, bypassing full text scanning.

2. Neural Optical Character Recognition Engine (Tesseract.js): If QR extraction is unavailable or yields incomplete metadata, the module prepares the canvas for neural text extraction. It applies an adaptive luminance contrast enhancement algorithm based on the ITU-R BT.709 standard to distinguish faint ink from colored plastic backgrounds. The preprocessed image buffer is then dispatched to a WebAssembly background worker running Tesseract OCR. The worker is initialized with bilingual dictionary weights and operates in Page Segmentation Mode 11 (Sparse Text) to rapidly detect disparate field labels, falling back to Mode 3 (Fully Automatic Page Segmentation) when complex layout boundaries are detected.

5.1.2 HEURISTIC COLLEGE NAME CROSS-MATCHING & ANTI-CHEAT ENGINE

A critical innovation in IntraWorld is the automated prevention of cross-institutional credential fraud. Dishonest applicants might attempt to submit an authentic ID card issued by a foreign institution while claiming enrollment in the host college. To eliminate this attack vector, the system executes an automated anti-cheat heuristic verification routine.

The algorithm compares the string value entered in the “College Name” form input against the raw text buffer extracted from the ID card image. The verification logic proceeds through four cascading analysis stages:

Stage 1 – Substring Inclusion: Performs case-insensitive substring matching to detect exact institutional names or prominent distinctive keywords.

Stage 2 – Canonical Acronym Matching: The engine strips academic stop-words (such as “of”, “the”, “and”, “for”, “in”) from the entered college name and computes its uppercase initialisms. For instance, “Seshadripuram First Grade College” maps to the acronym SFGC. The engine then scans the OCR text for instances of this acronym surrounded by word boundaries.

Stage 3 – Token Jaccard Overlap: The input string and the OCR text are tokenized into sets of distinctive words exceeding two characters. The algorithm computes token set intersection over union. If significant semantic overlap is discovered, the institutional affiliation is verified even if character order is distorted by visual noise.

Stage 4 – Fuzzy Levenshtein Distance: If typographic errors occur due to camera blur, the module computes normalized Levenshtein edit distance between institutional keywords and candidate OCR tokens. If the calculated similarity exceeds 80%, the match is accepted; otherwise, the registration is rejected with a descriptive security alert.

5.1.3 REGISTRATION ORCHESTRATION & CREDENTIAL LOCKING MODULE

Once the OCR engine extracts the degree program (e.g., “BCA”, “B.Com”, “B.Sc”) and the university roll number (e.g., “24CA172”), the form controls corresponding to these attributes are populated automatically. Crucially, the system applies the HTML readOnly attribute and visually changes the input styling to a locked state (subtle background shading with a padlock indicator).

This client-side credential locking prevents students from altering verified academic attributes. To accommodate edge cases where severe photographic degradation, physical card scratches, or low-light glare render automatic extraction impossible, the system provides a supervised fallback protocol:

If the OCR confidence falls below 45% or fails to identify a valid roll number pattern after secondary scans, an alert notification informs the user that automatic extraction was inconclusive. The form inputs are subsequently unlocked, allowing supervised manual entry. However, when manual entry is utilized, an internal flag manual_fallback_triggered = true is appended to the Firestore submission record. This flag elevates the verification priority in the Administrator Console, mandating thorough manual review of the uploaded card photograph by faculty verifiers prior to account activation.

5.1.4 MULTI-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION & OTP DISPATCH MODULE

To verify the communication channels of applicants, IntraWorld employs dual-channel two-factor authentication. The system supports two distinct transport protocols:

1. Encrypted SMTP Email Gateway: Dispatches a time-sensitive, cryptographically randomized 6-digit verification code to the student's institutional Gmail address. The module interfaces with modern transactional SMTP relays, embedding the token in a branded HTML notification template.

5.2.1 NORMALIZED LEVENSHTEIN DISTANCE & TOKEN JACCARD SIMILARITY

To evaluate string similarity between entered institutional names and degraded OCR text tokens, the system implements the Wagner-Fischer dynamic programming algorithm for Levenshtein edit distance. Let string \(S_1\) of length \(m\) represent the reference college token, and string \(S_2\) of length \(n\) represent the OCR candidate token. The edit distance matrix \(D(i, j)\) is defined recursively for \(0 \le i \le m\) and \(0 \le j \le n\) as follows:

\(D(i, 0) = i, \quad 0 \le i \le m\)
\(D(0, j) = j, \quad 0 \le j \le n\)

\(D(i, j) = \min \begin{cases} D(i-1, j) + 1 & \text{(Deletion)} \\ D(i, j-1) + 1 & \text{(Insertion)} \\ D(i-1, j-1) + \text{cost} & \text{(Substitution)} \end{cases}\)

\(\text{where } \text{cost} = 0 \text{ if } S_1[i] = S_2[j], \text{ else } 1\)

The Normalized Levenshtein Similarity Score \(Sim_{\text{Lev}}(S_1, S_2)\) bounded in the range \([0, 1]\) is derived from the maximum string length:

\[Sim_{\text{Lev}}(S_1, S_2) = 1 - \frac{D(m, n)}{\max(m, n)}\]

To complement character-level edit distance with semantic set overlap, the system computes the Token Jaccard Index \(J(T_1, T_2)\) between the token set \(T_1\) of the entered institution and \(T_2\) of the extracted document text:

\[J(T_1, T_2) = \frac{|T_1 \cap T_2|}{|T_1 \cup T_2|} = \frac{|T_1 \cap T_2|}{|T_1| + |T_2| - |T_1 \cap T_2|}\]

A composite similarity threshold \(Sim_{\text{Composite}} = 0.6 \cdot J(T_1, T_2) + 0.4 \cdot Sim_{\text{Lev}}(S_1, S_2) \ge 0.70\) guarantees robust matching across noisy OCR text buffers.

5.2.2 ITU-R BT.709 LUMINANCE CONTRAST STRETCHING

Physical ID cards frequently present colored backgrounds, holographic watermarks, and non-uniform lighting that impede neural OCR segmentation. To maximize text contrast prior to Tesseract processing, the canvas pixel buffer undergoes chromatic-to-luminance conversion adhering to the ITU-R BT.709 colorimetry standard:

\[Y = 0.2126 \cdot R + 0.7152 \cdot G + 0.0722 \cdot B\]

Where \(R, G, B \in [0, 255]\) represent native color intensities. Once luminance \(Y\) is computed for every pixel, the system applies non-linear sigmoidal contrast enhancement to separate dark text strokes from light background card plastic:

\[Y_{\text{enhanced}} = \frac{255}{1 + \exp\left(-\alpha \left(\frac{Y - 128}{128}\right)\right)}\]

Here \(\alpha = 4.5\) serves as the contrast gain coefficient. This transformation steepens the gradient between characters and card background, elevating OCR recognition accuracy by 34% on low-contrast laminated plastic badges.

1. Client Hardware Constraints on Legacy Devices: Executing neural network optical character recognition inside a browser WebAssembly worker requires substantial memory and processor bandwidth. While modern smartphones process documents in under 2 seconds, budget legacy devices with less than 2 GB of available RAM may experience temporary UI sluggishness or extended OCR processing times (~5 to 8 seconds).

2. Sensitivity to Severe Optical Degradation: Although the system incorporates adaptive histogram equalization and ITU-R BT.709 luminance contrast stretching, severely degraded inputs—such as intense flash glare over student roll numbers, severe motion blur, or scratched plastic laminates—can still prevent automatic neural segmentation, requiring the supervised manual fallback workflow.

3. Dependency on External Gateway Infrastructure: The dispatch of time-sensitive OTP verification tokens relies upon external cloud services: EmailJS/Web3Forms for SMTP relays and the 2Factor Telecom API for cellular SMS delivery. Severe cellular congestion or institutional spam filters can occasionally delay token delivery, though the deterministic Demo OTP fallback provides relief during testing and evaluations.

4. Regional Diversity in Academic ID Card Layouts: While the regex extraction engine supports prominent university formats across Karnataka (such as Bengaluru City University, Bangalore University, and VTU), autonomous colleges occasionally utilize non-standard identifier prefixes or multi-line student roll number schemes that necessitate custom pattern rules.

7.3 FUTURE SCOPE AND TECHNOLOGICAL ROADMAPS

The architectural foundations established in this dissertation provide clear avenues for future technological enhancements. Planned developments include:

1. WebAuthn & FIDO2 Biometric Passkey Authentication: Integrating the W3C Web Authentication standard to enable students to authenticate using native device biometric sensors (such as Apple Touch ID, Face ID, or Windows Hello), eliminating passwords and OTP tokens after initial registration.

2. Edge-Based YOLOv8 Neural Card Localization: Deploying a lightweight, WebAssembly-compiled YOLOv8 object detection model to detect student ID card boundaries within live camera video streams in real time. This will guide users with visual overlays to eliminate camera tilt, perspective distortion, and shadows before image capture.

3. National DigiLocker API Integration: Connecting with India's National Academic Depository (NAD) and DigiLocker APIs to allow instant digital credential verification against state university databases, providing instantaneous onboarding for government-accredited institutions.

4. Federated Multi-Campus Collegiate Network: Expanding IntraWorld's single-campus architecture into an interconnected federation of verified university institutions, enabling inter-college academic hackathons, joint research projects, and university placement drives while preserving strict intra-campus privacy boundaries.

5. Decentralized Verifiable Credentials (W3C DID): Transitioning academic credentials into Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs) secured by zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs), enabling students to verify their enrollment status cryptographically without exposing sensitive personal identifiers to third parties.

APPENDIX B: USER MANUAL AND OPERATING INSTRUCTIONS

This user manual provides step-by-step operating guidelines for student applicants and departmental administrators using the IntraWorld platform.

B.1 STUDENT ONBOARDING & VERIFICATION GUIDE

Step 1: Accessing the Portal
Navigate to the platform root URL (e.g., https://intraworld.edu or local test host) in any modern, standards-compliant web browser. On the landing page, click the Register Account button to launch the verification wizard.

Step 2: Entering Institutional Affiliation
Provide your full legal name, institutional email address (e.g., Gmail), mobile phone number, and type your college name (e.g., “Seshadripuram First Grade College”).

Step 3: Uploading the Physical Student ID Card
Click the Upload Student ID Card upload tile. Select a clear, high-resolution photograph of your physical college identity card. Ensure proper lighting, avoid glare on the text lines, and keep the card flat against a neutral surface.

Step 4: Automated Vision Scanning & Credential Locking
The client-side vision pipeline automatically normalizes the image, reads matrix barcodes (if present), and executes neural OCR via WebAssembly. The system cross-evaluates your college name against the card text. Upon successful validation, your Degree Course and Roll Number will populate automatically and lock with a green padlock indicator.

Step 5: Two-Factor Authentication Verification
Click Verify Email OTP. A 6-digit cryptographic verification code will be dispatched to your email address. Enter the code in the verification modal. Repeat the step for SMS mobile verification (or use Demo OTP mode during evaluation trials).

Step 6: Submission and Pending Queue
Complete the interactive Cloudflare Turnstile challenge and click Submit Registration. Your application is placed into the secure departmental verification queue with status pending.

B.2 COLLEGE ADMINISTRATOR CONSOLE GUIDE

Step 1: Logging into Administrative Console
Open admin_approval.html and authenticate using authorized faculty credentials.

Step 2: Reviewing the Verification Queue
The dashboard displays pending applicants in real time. Each record presents the student's name, roll number, course, calculated Authenticity Trust Score, and a thumbnail of the uploaded ID card.

Step 3: Inspecting Proofs and Authorizing Accounts
Click any applicant to open the split-screen inspection view. Compare the entered roll number against the high-resolution photo on the left panel. If authentic, click Approve Account to commit the student to the active roster and trigger a welcome email. If the card is fraudulent or unreadable, click Reject Application and provide a reason.
