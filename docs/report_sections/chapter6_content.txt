<h1>CHAPTER 6: SYSTEM TESTING</h1>

<h2>6.1 TEST STRATEGY AND METHODOLOGY</h2>
<p class=MsoNormal style="text-indent:0.5in;">
Software testing is a systematic investigation conducted to provide stakeholders with information regarding the quality, robustness, and contractual conformance of the software under development. For the <strong>IntraWorld</strong> ecosystem, quality assurance is especially critical because the platform enforces digital identity verification, client-side cryptographic processing, and administrative gatekeeping.
</p>
<p class=MsoNormal style="text-indent:0.5in;">
The testing methodology for IntraWorld is structured around the classic <strong>Software Quality Assurance V-Model Lifecycle</strong> (Figure 6.1). In this model, every phase of development is mapped directly to a corresponding verification and validation level:
</p>
<p class=MsoNormal>
<strong>1. Unit Testing:</strong> Validates isolated functional routines, including EXIF image orientation parsing, canvas color conversion, acronym generation, Levenshtein distance calculations, and regular expression pattern matchers.
</p>
<p class=MsoNormal>
<strong>2. Integration Testing:</strong> Evaluates data exchange across adjacent module boundaries, such as passing processed canvas pixel arrays from <code>jsQR</code> to <code>Tesseract.js</code>, routing verified form payloads into Google Cloud Firestore, and synchronizing SMTP/SMS OTP dispatch events.
</p>
<p class=MsoNormal>
<strong>3. System Testing:</strong> Examines the end-to-end functionality of the platform in unified deployment environments, verifying student onboarding, identity card validation, two-factor authentication, administrative approval routing, and campus social feeds.
</p>
<p class=MsoNormal>
<strong>4. Security &amp; Penetration Testing:</strong> Subjecting the platform to adversarial attack vectors, including automated bot submissions, SQL/NoSQL injection payloads, Stored Cross-Site Scripting (XSS), token replay attacks, and cross-college identity spoofing.
</p>
